Information Architecture Analysis

What goes on the card? We analyzed ER Triage protocols to determine the hierarchy of information. The card displays only the "Critical 5" data points to minimize cognitive load.

1. The Chief Complaint (50% Visual Weight)

The most critical data point. Why is the patient here? This drives the immediate triage category.

2. Safety Constraints (30% Visual Weight)

Allergies and History. These prevent medical errors during the initial stabilization phase (e.g., avoiding drugs that trigger reactions).

3. Admin Data (20% Visual Weight)

Name, Age, Insurance. Necessary for record-keeping but secondary to survival.

Revised Research Plan

Moving from "Allergy Card" to "Universal Medical Passport".

Phase 1: Analysis

ER Protocol Audit

Analyze admission forms from 3 major hospitals (VN/INTL) to identify the "Minimum Viable Data" set required for 90% of emergencies.

Phase 2: Standardization

Terminology Validation

Create a verified database of medical terms. Consult bilingual doctors to ensure "Chest Pain" maps to clinical "Cơn đau thắt ngực" not literal translations.

Phase 3: Development

Dual-View Prototype

Build the toggle interface (Patient EN / Doctor VN) demonstrated above. Focus on high contrast and "Glanceability" (3-second rule).

Phase 4: Validation

Simulated Pilot

Test with 10 ER nurses using simulated cards. Measure "Time to Understanding" vs. Google Translate.
